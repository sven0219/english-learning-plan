# Repository Rules

## Public Repository — No Company Information

This is a **public repository**. Never add company-related, confidential, internal, or identifying information to this repository.

This rule has the highest priority and applies to all content created by people, agents, scripts, templates, examples, and automated daily lessons.

### Prohibited content

Do not include real:

- Company, customer, partner, vendor, team, or employee names
- Project, application, service, repository, branch, or environment names
- Domains, URLs, IP addresses, cloud accounts, clusters, namespaces, or regions that identify a company
- Ticket numbers, pipeline names, deployment identifiers, incident details, or internal architecture
- Source code, configuration, commands, logs, screenshots, meeting notes, transcripts, or recordings from work
- Usernames, email addresses, credentials, tokens, secrets, certificates, or personal data
- Dates, metrics, errors, or combinations of facts that could indirectly identify an internal event

### Required content style

- Use only fictional and generic workplace scenarios.
- Use neutral placeholders such as `the application`, `the test environment`, `the project team`, and `the infrastructure team`.
- Invent all examples from scratch; never transform, summarize, or anonymize actual company material.
- Keep technical details generic and limited to what is needed for English practice.
- Before every commit, review the complete staged diff for direct and indirect company information.
- If there is any uncertainty about whether content is safe for a public repository, do not add it.

## Learning Content

- Daily passages should be 100–150 words at approximately A2–B1 level.
- Each passage should cover one fictional Internet or IT workplace meeting scenario.
- Include 3–5 reusable meeting or business phrases, substitution practice, comprehension questions, a retelling task, and spaced-review prompts.
- Reuse approximately 70% familiar language and introduce approximately 30% new language.
- Optimize for clear workplace communication, not complex grammar or native-like accents.

### Internet and IT topic scope

- Focus lessons on generic software development, APIs, application testing, cloud services, infrastructure, networking, databases, DevOps, CI/CD, releases, deployments, rollbacks, monitoring, performance, troubleshooting, incident reviews, information security, access control, change management, or technical project planning.
- Use vendor-neutral terms such as `the cloud platform`, `the source code repository`, `the test environment`, and `the monitoring system`.
- Keep scenarios technically plausible but simple enough for language practice.
- Treat “work-related” as a subject-area requirement only. Never use real work materials, facts, names, systems, timelines, metrics, errors, or events as input, even after anonymization.
- Balance reusable meeting language with a small amount of practical Internet and IT vocabulary.

### Business English and abbreviations

- Include common business communication for priorities, timelines, ownership, budgets, decisions, risks, negotiation, stakeholder alignment, polite disagreement, follow-up, and action items.
- Every lesson should include at least one reusable business sentence pattern. Add one or two common abbreviations only when they fit the scenario naturally.
- When an abbreviation first appears in a lesson, provide its full English form and a Chinese explanation.
- Explain whether an expression is mainly used in speech, email, or instant messaging, and note tone risks where relevant. For example, `ASAP` can sound demanding without context.
- Explain ambiguous abbreviations in context. For example, `POC` may mean `point of contact` or `proof of concept`, while `ETA` may refer to arrival, completion, or the next update.
- Use only widely recognized, vendor-neutral abbreviations such as `FYI`, `EOD`, `ETA`, `TBD`, `TBC`, `ASAP`, `OOO`, `PTO`, `KPI`, `ROI`, `SLA`, `BAU`, and `WIP`.
- Never invent, copy, or include company-specific or team-specific abbreviations.

## Repository Workflow

- Work on the `main` branch unless the user explicitly requests another workflow.
- Keep Markdown links valid and the repository easy to navigate.
- Do not commit generated audio, raw recordings, or private notes.
- Before committing, inspect the staged diff and confirm that it complies with the public-repository rule above.
